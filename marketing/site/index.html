A model proposes. Code checks the rules. You decide.

CashAsh researches a small personal stock portfolio every Sunday evening, proposes a handful of constrained actions, and sends them to your phone. Nothing is bought or sold until you do it yourself, in your broker's app.

Read the docs Source on GitHub

Open source. Runs on your own machine. Your holdings never leave it.

One Sunday evening, in order.

  1. 1Sync

    Prices, FX, earnings dates and consensus. A failed fetch keeps last week's prices, marks them stale, and the run carries on without proposing trades.

  2. 2Triage

    One call over the whole portfolio, ranking holdings by what changed in each thesis rather than by what moved. The ones passed over are recorded too.

  3. 3Research

    Questions are planned from your own thesis, answered from evidence, and judged. A weakened thesis gets a proposed revision beside it. Yours is never rewritten.

  4. 4Recommend

    BUY, ADD, HOLD, TRIM, EXIT or REVIEW. Then the rules run, in code, and say no where they must. Every refusal is kept.

  5. 5Report

    To Telegram, with each finding, citation and refusal attached. A stage that failed or was skipped is shown as exactly that.

  6. 6You

    Monday, after a night's sleep. Approve, reject or later. Approval reserves the cash. The trade is still yours to place, and then to record.

The rules are code, not a request.

A prompt asking a model to respect a position limit is a polite suggestion. So the limits are applied afterwards, deterministically, to whatever the model proposed. That is the only reason a model is allowed near the decision at all.

  • No position above {{MAX_POSITION_WEIGHT_PCT}}% of the portfolio.The whole batch is checked together, so five small buys cannot do what one large one may not.
  • A single trade is capped at €{{MAX_NEW_TRADE_EUR}}. New money in a week is capped at €{{MAX_WEEKLY_ALLOCATION_EUR}}.An approved buy reserves its cash until it is executed or rejected, so a rerun cannot spend the same euro twice.
  • Only cash that is actually there can fund a buy.Planned monthly contributions are noted, never spent.
  • EXIT needs a condition you wrote down to have happened.A falling price or a bad week is not one. Anything less becomes TRIM.
  • A price older than {{PRICE_STALE_AFTER_DAYS}} days, or a missing FX rate, blocks any trade.A review can still go through. Money cannot.
  • Advice expires after {{RECOMMENDATION_EXPIRY_DAYS}} days.A decision cannot be recorded against advice a later run has withdrawn.

Every default lives in src/config.py beside a note on how it was chosen. main.py process prints the ones in force.

Every finding carries a label.

  • sourcedCites a supplied evidence item by ID and copies an exact excerpt. The URL and date come from the item, not from the model. An unknown ID or an invented quote demotes the finding to unanswered.
  • backgroundContext the model brought with it. Held to nothing, and never enough on its own to move money.
  • unansweredStays unanswered, in storage and on your phone. An open question is a finding.

A quote check proves the excerpt exists. It does not prove the conclusion follows. That part is still reading.

Your thesis is yours. Research that concludes it has weakened, improved or broken writes a proposed version beside the active one and stops. You accept it, or you don't, and the baseline the system is judged against stays the one you wrote.

It will probably cost more than it earns.

The portfolio behind this is about EUR 1,400 across 14 positions. A weekly research pipeline over several models can cost more in a year than any excess return that capital could produce. That is the accepted trade: the point is a better research habit and an interesting system, not a return.

So models route cheap by default, and the expensive ones are opt-in and metered per stage. And nothing a model says carries investment authority. A model's stated confidence is not a probability, and CashAsh will not store or display it as one.

The evals measure the process, because that is what can be measured here:

  • the output has the shape it was asked for;
  • every claim that says it has a source actually has one;
  • the rules were obeyed, and each refusal was recorded;
  • no figure was invented;
  • a rerun gives the same answer.

What they cannot measure is whether the advice was good. Fourteen holdings a week will never be a sample, and replaying history through a model that has already read the ending measures leakage, not judgement. Outcomes are still recorded, beside the same money left in a {{BENCHMARK_NAME}} tracker. Recorded, never claimed.

Start from a screenshot of your broker.

The seed is a snapshot you type in by hand: ticker, quantity, value and the broker's blended return. Cost basis is derived from those, and the total is reconciled against the figure your broker showed, so a typo cannot quietly become your cost basis. The file lives in your home directory and is never committed.

# one profile, one snapshot, one database
uv run python main.py profile add adam --telegram-id 123456789 --operator
cp seed_snapshot.example.toml ~/Documents/cash-ash/profiles/adam/seed_snapshot.toml
uv run python main.py init --dry-run   # does it reconcile?
uv run python main.py init

# then, each week
uv run python main.py weekly --telegram

Every command, the weekly cycle and the evidence format are in the docs. What is not built yet is on the roadmap.
